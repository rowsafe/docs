title: FAQ
description: Short answers to common questions about Rowsafe.
---

<Accordions type="multiple">
<Accordion title="Do I need to know PostgreSQL?">

No. You answer a few questions when you install Rowsafe, and then it runs by itself: backups, the weekly Proof and monitoring. When something needs you, Rowsafe says what's wrong in plain words, and most problems have an **Apply fix** button. Getting data back is buttons too: see [Your first restore](/docs/first-restore) for a practice run. New words are explained in the [Glossary](/docs/glossary).

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="What are Rewind, Proof, Pulse and Guard?">

The four parts of Rowsafe:

- **Rewind**: every change saved, so you can restore to any second, or to a **Mark** (a named restore point) you set before a risky change. See [Restore a database](/docs/guides/restore).
- **Proof**: every week Rowsafe restores a copy of your database and checks it. See [Proof](/docs/concepts/restore-drills).
- **Pulse**: the health score, alerts, insights, slow queries and the weekly email. See [Pulse](/docs/guides/monitoring).
- **Guard**: the safety net for AI agents. See [Guard](/docs/guides/ai-agents).

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="What happens if I delete rows by mistake?">

You get them back, and production keeps running. In the dashboard, open the database, then **Rewind**. [Find the moment](/docs/guides/find-the-moment) shows the exact second of the delete. **Restore a copy** of the database as it was just before, compare it with production, and **bring back** the missing rows. Rows written since stay as they are. See [Restore a database](/docs/guides/restore), and practise once with [Your first restore](/docs/first-restore).

Every change is saved within about a minute, so you can go back to any second of about the last two weeks (by default).

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="What if I use an AI coding agent?">

Set up [Guard](/docs/guides/ai-agents). Before a migration or a risky command, the agent checks that the database can be restored and saves a **Mark**, a named moment you can rewind to. The Claude Code plugin also sets a Mark by itself before destructive commands. Agents can also [preview a migration](/docs/guides/preview-migrations) on a copy, and test against a masked [safe copy](/docs/guides/safe-copies) instead of production.

Agents never restore, rewind, restart PostgreSQL or apply fixes on their own. They can only ask, and nothing changes until an owner or admin clicks **Approve** in the dashboard ([Approvals](/docs/guides/approvals)). If something breaks, you decide what to do.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Can Rowsafe read my data?">

No. Backups are encrypted on your server before they are uploaded to your bucket, and the passphrase (and your bucket keys) never leave your server. Rowsafe stores metadata only: names, sizes, settings, task logs and metrics. See the [security model](/docs/security).

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Does it slow down my database?">

Barely, and it is built to stay out of the way:

- **Monitoring** opens one short session every minute, over the local socket, and runs a few cheap queries. Each has a 2-second timeout, so a busy server gets a gap in the graphs, never a pile of monitoring queries. See [what the agent collects](/docs/guides/monitoring#what-the-agent-collects).
- **Saving every change** copies files PostgreSQL has already written.
- **Backups** run at low CPU and IO priority. **Proof**, Rewind copies and safe copies run at low CPU and IO priority too.
- **The agent** gets at most half the default CPU and IO weight, and if the server runs out of memory, the kernel stops it before PostgreSQL.

Proof and copies need free disk: about 1.3 × the database size plus 1 GB.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Does Rowsafe ever restart my database?">

Never on its own. Settings that need a restart (`archive_mode`, and `wal_level` if it is `minimal`) are written with `ALTER SYSTEM`. The installer asks whether to restart now: usually a few seconds, and open connections are dropped. Say no, and restart when it suits you: Rowsafe notices by itself.

If you allowed it during install, admins can also restart PostgreSQL later from the dashboard, always with a confirmation. The same permission lets them [rewind the whole database](/docs/guides/restore#rewind-the-whole-database), which stops PostgreSQL while Rowsafe restores it. AI agents can't do either on their own: they can only ask, and it happens when an owner or admin approves it ([Approvals](/docs/guides/approvals)).

Two things restart or stop PostgreSQL without a click, and only if you turn them on yourself: [automatic minor updates](/docs/guides/updates#minor-updates) (Sundays at 03:00 in your time zone) and [automatic failover](/docs/guides/standby#automatic-failover) to a standby. Both are off by default.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Which databases are supported?">

- **PostgreSQL 13 to 18**, fully supported: on Debian 12/13 and Ubuntu 22.04/24.04 servers (amd64 or arm64), and in Docker with the official `postgres` images 14 to 18.
- **MySQL 8.0 and 8.4, and MariaDB 10.6, 10.11 and 11.4**, supported: on the same servers and in Docker, with every Rowsafe feature. See [Protect MySQL or MariaDB](/docs/guides/mysql) and its limits.
- **MongoDB 6.0, 7.0 and 8.0**, supported: replica sets (one member is enough) on the same servers and in Docker with the official `mongo` images; not sharded clusters. Every feature except connection pooling, which MongoDB's drivers do themselves. See [Protect MongoDB](/docs/guides/mongodb) and its limits.
- **ClickHouse 24.8 and newer**, supported: single servers on the same systems and in Docker with the official `clickhouse/clickhouse-server` images (25.8 to 26.8). Every feature, restore to any second included (every part ClickHouse writes is copied as it appears). See [Protect ClickHouse](/docs/guides/clickhouse) and its limits.
- **Redis 7.0 and newer, and Valkey 7.2 and newer**, supported: standalone servers (with or without replicas) on Debian 12/13, Ubuntu 24.04 (on 22.04, with Redis's own packages) and in Docker with the official `redis` and `valkey/valkey` images; not Redis Cluster or servers managed by Sentinel. Backups, restore to any second, Marks, Proof, Rewind, Pulse with its fixes, security checks, Tuning, updates and upgrades, logs, users and recommendations, restart, files, the second copy, standby servers, clones, moving in, Find the moment and safe copies; no connection pooling (Redis doesn't need it) or migration previews (Redis has no schema). See [Protect Redis or Valkey](/docs/guides/redis) and its limits.
- **SQLite 3**, supported: the database files your apps open, on the local disk of the same servers, and in Docker volumes (the agent as a sidecar). Backups, restore to any second (in WAL mode), Marks, Proof, Rewind and Pulse; no features that need a database server, and a few others not yet. See [Protect SQLite](/docs/guides/sqlite) and its limits.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="How does Rowsafe back up Redis or Valkey?">

The agent connects to Redis the way a replica does. Redis sends it a full snapshot (Redis forks to make it, as it does for its own snapshots and for any new replica), then every change as it happens. The agent stamps each change with the second it arrived and uploads the changes about every minute, encrypted on your server. So you can restore to about a second, or exactly to a Mark. Redis lists the agent among its replicas as `rowsafe-agent`; it never serves anything.

If the server refuses that link (replication not allowed for Rowsafe's user, or `min-replicas-to-write` in use), backups are scheduled snapshots only, and the dashboard says so. See [How Redis and Valkey backups work](/docs/concepts/redis).

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Does Rowsafe slow down my SQLite app?">

Barely. The agent opens the file with SQLite's own locking, as one more connection, and only takes SQLite's write lock for a few milliseconds at a time. Backups use SQLite's online backup API, made for copying a database while it is in use. In WAL mode, your app's writes never wait for Rowsafe's reads; in rollback-journal mode, a write can wait a moment while Rowsafe reads, as it would behind any other reader. Only two things you ask for make writes wait: rewinding the whole file (a few seconds) and **Give free space back** (`VACUUM`; the confirmation says about how long). See [Safe next to your app](/docs/guides/sqlite#safe-next-to-your-app).

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Does Rowsafe change my SQLite file?">

Not on its own. Backups only read it. Rowsafe checkpoints the `-wal` file once it has copied the changes in it, which is what SQLite does on its own anyway, so the `-wal` file doesn't keep growing. Everything else happens only when someone clicks and confirms: bring back rows, rewind the whole file, and the fixes Pulse offers.

At install, root gives the agent access to the file, its `-wal` and `-shm` files and their folder (with an ACL, or a group), and prints what it changed. The file's owner never changes, and everyone else's access stays as it was.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Does my SQLite database need WAL mode?">

For restores to any second and Marks, yes. WAL mode (`journal_mode=WAL`) is the default in Rails 8 and common elsewhere. Without it, Rowsafe takes a full backup every day and you can restore to those backups. Pulse offers **Turn on continuous backups**, which switches the file to WAL mode: your app keeps working, and `-wal` and `-shm` files appear next to the database. See [How SQLite backups work](/docs/concepts/sqlite#every-change-in-wal-mode).

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="My SQLite file is in a Docker volume. Does that work?">

Yes. The installer on the host finds SQLite files that apps in containers have open. Or run the agent as a sidecar in your compose project, with the app's volume mounted read-write and the agent running as the same user as your app. See [Protect SQLite](/docs/guides/sqlite#docker).

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Can Rowsafe protect a SQLite file on a network drive?">

No. Rowsafe refuses SQLite files on NFS, SMB/CIFS, sshfs and similar network filesystems: their file locks aren't reliable, so neither SQLite nor Rowsafe can safely share the file with your app there. Keep the file on the server's local disk.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="What counts as a database?">

One PostgreSQL cluster: one server instance, on one host and port. Every database inside it is backed up and checked together.

For SQLite, one database file. A server with several SQLite files has several databases in Rowsafe.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Which storage can I use?">

Any S3-compatible bucket you own: Cloudflare R2, Backblaze B2, Amazon S3, Wasabi, DigitalOcean Spaces, or self-hosted storage such as MinIO. The installer walks you through it; see [Set up backup storage](/docs/guides/storage). Your own bucket is billed by your provider, not by Rowsafe. With Cloudflare R2, the first 10 GB are free.

[Rowsafe Storage](/docs/guides/rowsafe-storage), a bucket run by Rowsafe with nothing to set up, is coming soon. It isn't available yet.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="How much data can I lose?">

At most about a minute of changes, usually less. PostgreSQL archives each WAL segment as it fills, and at least every minute (`archive_timeout = 60`). A nightly `pg_dump` can lose up to 24 hours.

Databases set up before this was the default archive at least every 5 minutes. **Health** shows "Backups can be up to 5 minutes behind" for them, and **Apply fix** changes it without a restart.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="How far back can I restore?">

From the start of your oldest kept full backup to the last archived change. By default Rowsafe keeps 2 weekly full backups: about two weeks. Change it in the database's settings in the dashboard, or with `rowsafe set NAME --retention-full N`.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Does Rowsafe restore for me?">

Yes, in one click, on the database's own server. On the **Rewind** tab, pick a second or a Mark and:

- **restore a copy** next to production, compare it with production, and **bring back** the rows you lost. Production keeps running;
- or **rewind the whole database** to that moment, for the worst case. You can undo it for 7 days.

See [Restore a database](/docs/guides/restore). Rewinding the whole database in Docker needs the optional [container control service](/docs/guides/docker#let-rowsafe-restart-the-container), and tables need a primary key to bring rows back. When the server itself is lost, you restore by hand onto a new one with pgBackRest, as the same guide describes. Proof, the weekly restore test, runs the same kind of restore, so you know it works and how long it takes.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="What if I lose the encryption passphrase?">

Then nobody can restore those backups, Rowsafe included. Store the passphrase in your secret manager, and in a second place, before the first backup exists.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="What happens if Rowsafe is down?">

PostgreSQL keeps archiving its changes to your bucket by itself, so point-in-time recovery keeps working. Scheduled backups, Proof and alerts resume when the service is back.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="What happens if the agent stops?">

The same: PostgreSQL keeps archiving. Backups, Proof and metrics pause, you get the `agent_offline` alert after 5 minutes, and the database stops counting as protected after 10.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Can I keep my existing backups?">

Yes, and you should, for a while. Run your old job next to Rowsafe for 30 days: four passing Proof runs and one restore you did yourself, then retire it.

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Does it do replicas and failover?">

Yes, for PostgreSQL. A [standby](/docs/guides/standby) is a second server that Rowsafe keeps in sync from your backups. You can read from it, and promote it when the primary dies, by hand or automatically (off by default). One standby per database, on Linux servers with systemd; the standby's agent is installed natively, not in Docker. To back up, adopt the primary: a standby can't be adopted on its own. Redis and Valkey standbys are real replicas Rowsafe sets up ([Redis and Valkey](/docs/guides/standby#redis-and-valkey)).

</Accordion>
<Accordion title="Is it open source?">

Everything that runs on your servers is: the agent, the CLI (including the MCP server and the Claude Code plugin) and the installer, under Apache-2.0 on [GitHub](https://github.com/rowsafe/rowsafe). These docs are [open source](https://github.com/rowsafe/docs) too, under CC BY 4.0. The hosted service and dashboard are proprietary.

</Accordion>
